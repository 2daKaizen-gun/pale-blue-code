## Scope

sub-11-3 measured how often the hunt cries wolf: invert every cached curve so no real dip
remains, run the full chain, count survivors — zero across five, even where inverted noise
reached sig 23.57 (the `edge` discriminator caught it). That is **specificity**. Its twin is
**completeness**: what could the hunt have seen? A hunt that finds nothing because it is blind
is worthless; one that finds nothing *after* proving it would have caught a 0.5%-deep transit
is a real null. sub-11-4 measures that, and measures the price of the very discriminators that
buy the specificity — the `edge` rule that kills the loudest false alarm also kills a real
transit that crosses near a data gap.

- **issue** (`3aeb411`): `docs/issues/issue-11-4-body.md`, the plan and success criteria (#52).
- **L1 — injection + recovery** (`178eba2`): `inject_transit` presses a trapezoid of known
  depth into a real curve; `recover_through_hunt` runs the whole chain and reports not just
  whether the injected event is promoted but WHERE it dies — search / held / insignificant /
  a named discriminator / promoted.
- **L2 — the completeness map** (`1b12568`): `scripts/measure_monotransit_completeness.py`,
  the depth x position sweep (below).
- **L3 — per-star sensitivity floor** (`62da902`): `sensitivity_depth_floor` inverts the
  red-noise null (`_control_depths` / `_control_scatter`, factored out of `significance_red`

  every searched star carries `depth_floor` into the ledger.
- **L4 — the surface states its sensitivity** (`5044be6`): `sensitivityFloor` (median per-star
  floor over searched stars) + `sensitivityCaption` render the empty hunt's detection scale as

## The completeness map (L2)

Inject a trapezoidal single transit of known depth into the two genuine single-sector TESS
curves, at 12 sites per cell, and run the full chain. Mid-segment (clear of any edge) vs near a
segment join (within 0.5 d). Recovery fraction, and the stage that dropped every non-recovery:

```
POOLED MID-SEGMENT RECOVERY               depth   recovered   frac
  (the completeness curve)                0.20%      0/24       0%   (all lost at search)
                                          0.40%     17/24      71%
                                          0.60%     23/24      96%
                                          0.80%     24/24     100%
                                          1.20%     23/24      96%

DEPTH FLOOR (first depth with >=50% mid-segment recovery):  0.40%
```

Read it straight: the full hunt recovers reliably at **~0.40% depth** and deeper, and nothing
at 0.20% — every shallow injection is lost at the **search** stage (the dip never surfaces),
not at significance. Above 0.6% recovery is essentially complete. This independently reproduces
sub-11-2's significance-recovery floor (0.4% at 78%), now measured through the *whole* chain.

### The completeness cost of the edge discriminator

The same injections, mid-segment vs beside a segment join:

```
                       0.40%   0.60%   0.80%   1.20%
  tic-7318157  mid       75%     92%    100%     92%
               edge      58%     67%     75%     25%
  tic-7187543  mid       67%    100%    100%    100%
               edge      33%     25%     25%     42%
```

Near a segment edge, recovery collapses — a real, deep, significant transit is rejected because
its `t0` sits within `_EDGE_CLEAR` of a join (the tally shows `edge:` claiming most of the
losses, e.g. `edge:9` at 0.60% on tic-7187543). This is the measured **completeness cost** of
the guard that, in sub-11-3, rejected the loudest false alarm (inverted sig 23.57). Specificity
near edges is bought with sensitivity near edges — an honest, quantified trade, not a free lunch.
A single transit that happens to cross beside a data gap is the blind spot we accept to keep the
false-alarm rate at zero.

## The per-star sensitivity floor, and the gap that teaches (L3)

`sensitivity_depth_floor` reads each curve's own red-noise null and inverts it: the depth that
scores exactly `_SIG_MIN_MONO` is the shallowest single transit that curve's noise would let
clear the significance cut. On the single-sector curves it lands near **0.21%**.

But the completeness map recovers reliably only near **0.40%**. The factor-~2 gap is the whole
lesson of this sub-phase: the analytic floor is **significance-limited** (a necessary
condition), while real recovery also needs the **search** to surface the dip — and at the
shallow end that is the binding constraint (every 0.20% miss died at `search`, not at
significance). So `depth_floor` is reported and worded as a **floor** — "a single transit
shallower than this could not clear the cut" — never as "anything deeper would be found." The
honest empty-state states the floor; it does not promise completeness above it.

## Honest boundary (carried forward)

The measured floor (~0.40%) and the analytic floor (~0.21%) are both quoted at a single
central-transit duration on the *single-sector* population — the honest monotransit target. The
completeness cost near edges is a real blind spot the hunt accepts to keep the false-alarm rate
at zero. And the per-star floor is a **detection** floor, not a completeness guarantee. What the
two scoreboards together now give — specificity from sub-11-3, sensitivity here — is the ability
to read an empty hunt honestly: not "nothing is there," but "nothing deeper than this floor
crossed once, and here is how sure we are." The next question is not about the tool but about
the sky it points at: are the frontier pool's stars even ones where a detectable dip would be a
*planet*? That is sub-11-5.

## Result

The hunt now knows its own reach. It recovers single transits to ~0.40% depth mid-segment,
loses them near segment joins (the measured price of the edge guard), and reports for every star
the shallowest dip its noise would admit. Paired with sub-11-3's zero false alarms, an empty
result is no longer silence — it is a measurement with error bars.
