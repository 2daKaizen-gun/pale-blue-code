## The disposition ledger

Every star gets the disposition of its furthest-reaching event, closest-to-a-candidate first:

| disposition     | meaning                                                            |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `candidate`     | cleared significance **and** every discriminator — a survivor     |
| `rejected`      | significant, but a named discriminator caught it                  |
| `held`          | an event, but too little baseline to judge significance (`None`)  |
| `insignificant` | judged, and below the measured significance floor                 |
| `no_event`      | the search found no dip clearing the structural floors           |
| `not_fetched`   | no light curve cached — nothing searched, said plainly           |

A survivor is promoted **only** when `candidate=True` (significant AND all discriminators pass),
so the survivor list and the ledger can never disagree.

## The false-alarm scoreboard (L2)

`scripts/measure_monotransit_false_alarm.py`, re-run live for this Check. Two nulls on real
TESS data, no injection — the honest question is how often the whole chain manufactures a
survivor from noise shape alone.

**Cross-star significance null** — `significance_red` at 200 random non-transit epochs, on the
two genuine single-sector curves (the population the hunt targets: one sector, one possible
transit). sub-11-2 calibrated the 5.0 floor on one star; this confirms it holds on the other.

```
              star    span       N    95th    99th     max
       tic-7318157    24.6   15698    1.59    2.14    2.86
       tic-7187543    24.6   15698    1.81    2.23    2.69
```

Noise tops out at **2.86 / 2.69** over 200 epochs each — `_SIG_MIN_MONO = 5.0` sits well above
both, exactly as the single-star calibration promised. (The three multi-sector caches are
concatenations spanning thousands of days, skipped here — their edges are sector joins, the
subject of the inversion test below.)

**Full-chain inversion test** — flip each curve (`flux -> 2*median - flux`). A real transit is
a dip; inverted it becomes a bump the dip-search cannot see, so every real dip and downward
glitch is removed while the noise, the red-noise wobble, and the segment structure are
preserved exactly. Any survivor the full chain still finds is a false alarm from noise shape
alone — same star, same noise, no planet.

```
              star    span  max sig  survivors
       tic-7318157    24.6    -0.00          0
       tic-7187543    24.6    -0.01          0
     tic-102029734  2850.4    23.57          0
     tic-253825414  2547.7     4.59          0
     tic-469906369  2879.2      nan          0
------------------------------------------------
FALSE ALARMS (full chain, inverted nulls): 0 across 5 curves.
```

**Zero.** The load-bearing row is `tic-102029734`: inverted, noise alone drives the significance
to **23.57** — nearly 5x the threshold — and still **no survivor**, because the `edge`
discriminator rejects it (segment joins ring exactly there, up or down). This is the sub-11-2
lesson made a measurement: **the significance cut is necessary but not sufficient; the
discriminators, edge first, are what stop the loudest false alarm.** A hunt cutting on
significance alone would have cried wolf at 23.57.

## Live hunt over the pool

`hunt_monotransit` over the five cached real curves — the honest result on stars never selected
for having a lone transit:

```
              star           disp   n      sig  failed
       TIC 7318157  insignificant   1    -0.07  []
       TIC 7187543       no_event   0     None  []
     TIC 102029734       rejected   1    14.23  [edge]
     TIC 253825414  insignificant   1    -0.00  []
     TIC 469906369       rejected   2    11.68  [edge, symmetry]
------------------------------------------------------------
survivors: 0
```

Zero survivors, and the ledger says *why* for each: two dips too shallow to be significant, one
star with no event at all, and the two loud dips (14.23, 11.68) both **rejected by
discriminators** — `102029734` on `edge`, `469906369` on `edge` and `symmetry` (a one-sided
ramp no central transit leaves). The two genuine single-sector stars produce nothing real; the
loud events live on the multi-sector caches' sector joins. Exactly the shape sub-11-2 predicted.

## Honest boundary (carried forward)

The hunt searches only **cached** light curves; the frontier pool's uncached stars are reported
`not_fetched`, not silently dropped. The five cached curves were never selected for having a
lone transit, so zero survivors is the correct and expected result — the point of sub-11-3 was
to build the hunt and the scoreboard, not to find a planet. What the scoreboard buys is the
ability to state a *future* real candidate with a known chance-of-noise: the full chain
produced zero false alarms across five inverted nulls, and rejected a 23.57-sigma noise event
on its shape. The next question is the twin of this one — not how often the hunt cries wolf, but
what it could have seen when it stayed silent. That is sub-11-4 (completeness), which measures
the depth floor and the completeness cost of the very discriminators that bought this
specificity.
