## Scope

sub-11-3 measured the hunt's false-alarm rate (zero across five nulls); sub-11-4 measured its
completeness (floor ~0.40%); sub-11-5 turned to the sky and ranked the pool by what a detection
would MEAN — 16 of 50 stars small enough that a detectable dip could be a planet, 34 giants where
it would be a star. But the hunt itself did not know this. `GET /api/monotransit/hunt` searched
the whole pool and ranked its survivors by significance, so a loud dip on a 10 R_sun giant would
be promoted and top-ranked exactly like a dip on a dwarf — even though sub-11-5 had already proved
that dip cannot be a planet. sub-11-6 makes the hunt honor the triage.

- **issue** (`66c35fe`): `docs/issues/issue-11-6-body.md`, the plan and success criteria (#54).
- **L1 — the join** (`1499f7b`): `pool_assessment.size_class_by_name()` keys the sub-11-5 verdict
  by the hunt's star name; `hunt_monotransit(names, size_index=None)` stamps every ledger entry and
  survivor with `{size_class, min_planet_rjup, min_planet_rearth}`. Absent, or a name not in the
  pool, carries nulls — the same honesty as an unfetched star, never a guessed class.
- **L2 — the ranking** (`e657e40`): survivors sorted `(size_rank, -significance)` — plausible <

- **L3 — the surface** (`423d796`): the hunt page carries the verdict — a plausible survivor is
  highlighted and names its minimum detectable planet radius (the real prize); a too_big one is
  still shown but framed as star-sized and dimmed. `survivorSizeNote` is pure and tested. **Not**
  added to `phases.ts`.

## What changed, and why it is honest (L1–L2)

The size class is not re-derived here — it is the *same* `assess_pool` verdict from sub-11-5, keyed
by star name so there is exactly one definition of plausible / too_big in the codebase. The hunt
joins it, it does not recompute it.

The ranking change is the crux. Before, survivors sorted by significance alone; a giant's deeper,
louder dip would top the list. Now:

```
survivor sort key = (size_rank, -significance)     size_rank: plausible 0 < too_big 1 < unknown/None 2
```

Demonstrated on a two-star hunt (test): a too_big giant with a deep, quiet transit scores
**significance 58.2**, a plausible dwarf with a shallow, noisier one scores **38.8** — and the
plausible dwarf still ranks **first**. A significance-only sort would have led with the giant; the
triage-aware sort leads with the star where a detection could be a planet. The louder signal did
not win.

Two honesty guards held:

- **Report, do not hide.** A too_big survivor is still a `candidate` if it cleared every gate — the
  event is reported, tagged, and ranked, never suppressed. The verdict changes the ranking and the
  label, not whether the tool admits what it saw.
- **Never guess a class.** A star with no triage (no size_index, or a name absent from the pool)
  carries `size_class: null` and null radii, exactly like an unfetched star's honest blank — not a
  fabricated "unknown-is-fine."

## The surface (L3)

The hunt page reads the verdict off each survivor. A plausible survivor wears an emerald chip
"행성 크기 · 검출 시 ≥ X.XX R♃" and its card is highlighted; a too_big survivor wears a muted
"검출돼도 별 (너무 큼)" chip and its card is dimmed; a star with no triage reads "크기 미상". The
intro states the ranking rule plainly — survivors are ordered by whether a detection could be a
world, "거성에서 뜬 깊은 딥은 별이지 세계가 아니니까요". The empty-handed state is unchanged and
still first-class.

## Honest boundary (carried forward)

The size verdict rests on the sub-11-5 fiducial floor (0.40%) and the catalog stellar radius; a
wrong radius moves a star across the plausible/too_big boundary, and the class is a *size* gate,
not a planethood claim — a plausible survivor is planet-*sized* and would still need a mass. The
ranking is deterministic and offline, but it only ranks survivors the hunt actually produces, and
the hunt still only searches cached curves. What sub-11-6 closes is the last gap between the
instrument and the sky: the hunt no longer reports a giant's dip as if it could be a world, and it
points its ranking at the 16 stars where a single-transit detection could be one. The tool now
knows its reach (sub-11-4), its false-alarm rate (sub-11-3), and where that reach is worth
spending (sub-11-5) — and, here, acts on all three at once. The next step is the north star itself:
actually fetch the plausible dwarfs and run the real hunt.

## Result

The pool hunt is no longer size-blind. A candidate is ranked and named by whether a detection there
could be a planet at all — the plausible few first and highlighted, the star-sized many reported
but named as what they are. The three measurements the hunt has been accumulating — how deep it
sees, how often it lies, and where a detection could be a world — now converge on a single honest
ranked result.
