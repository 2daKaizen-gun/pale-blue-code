# Check: sub-11-2 — Single-transit search, the first brick

**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Branch:** main
**Verdict:** PASS (the monotransit primitive ships end to end; the threshold is
measured not asserted; the live sweep over five real stars finds zero survivors —
and the one loud false alarm is caught by a discriminator, not the significance cut)

---

## Scope

BLS finds a planet by its **repetition**: fold on a trial period, stack the dips, gain
signal from every orbit. A planet that crosses its star only once inside a 27-day TESS
sector has nothing to fold — one dip, k = 1 — so BLS discards it, and the sub-11-1
significance precondition (which measures depth against the scatter *between* transits) is
blind to it by design. That single event is the frontier
`docs/vision/finding-our-own-planet.md` points at: what the machines miss.

sub-11-2 lays the primitive underneath a search for it. It does not fold. It scores one
place in the light curve against the shape a single transit would leave — a matched filter —
asks how far that dip stands above **correlated** noise, and then guards the survivor with
discriminators built for a lone event, none of which is one of the four repeat-transit
gates. This is a backend brick; there is no frontier surface yet (that is sub-11-3).

- **issue** (`eb9a6aa`): `docs/issues/issue-11-2-body.md`, the plan and success criteria (#50).
- **L1 — template + SES** (`bd3055e`): `transit_shape`/`transit_model` (trapezoid) and
  `matched_filter_score` — best-fit depth over its own white-noise uncertainty.
- **L2 — sliding search** (`4b089c3`): `search()` slides the filter over a (duration × t0)
  grid, dedups each dip to its strongest hit, and drops any dip with a comparable-depth
  twin — repetition is BLS's job.
- **L3 — red-noise significance** (`bc6ee62`): `significance_red()` normalizes the dip
  against the empirical scatter of same-duration depths elsewhere; `_SIG_MIN_MONO = 5.0`,
  measured (below). Returns `None` when there is too little baseline — judgment held.
- **L4 — lone-event discriminators** (`a218ebb`): `discriminators()` — edge, coherence,
  flare, symmetry. Each a named rule with a physical reason, so a rejection says *why*.
- **L5 — reachable endpoint** (`40ac8f9`): `GET /api/monotransit/{planet_name}` →
  `run_monotransit`, the full lc → detrend → search → significance → discriminators pass;
  424 when the light curve is not cached.

## L3: the threshold is measured, not asserted

`scripts/measure_monotransit_significance.py`, re-run live for this Check on the sub-10-3
noise case `TIC 7318157` (a real single-sector TESS curve, N = 15698, span 24.6 d, BLS
power 0.0004 — no real planet). The **null** is `significance_red` at 300 random
non-transit epochs: what correlated noise alone manufactures at the transit timescale.
The **recovered** rows inject one trapezoidal transit of known depth at a clean epoch,
run the search, and read the significance of the recovered event.

```
NULL (noise only, 300 epochs)          RECOVERED (40 trials each)
  50th pct   -0.04                        depth  recov%   z p10   z p50   z p90
  95th pct    1.69                        0.001     0%   (nothing recovered)
  99th pct    2.14                        0.002     2%    5.60    5.60    5.60
  max         2.86                        0.004    78%    6.52    8.11    9.13
                                          0.008   100%   13.81   15.22   17.28
                                          0.016   100%   27.51   30.90   33.08
```

Read it straight: noise tops out at **2.86** over 300 epochs; the shallowest transit the
search recovers with any reliability — 0.4 % deep, 78 % recovered — lands at **6.52** (p10)
and above. Between the loudest noise and the signal floor is an empty gap, and
**`_SIG_MIN_MONO = 5.0`** sits in it: ~2 above the loudest noise (false alarms ~nil), below
anything the search can actually recover. Measured, the way `_SIG_MIN` (4.0) and
`_N_MIN_TRANSITS` (8) were in sub-11-1 and sub-10-3 — not a round number picked by taste.

The 0.2 % row (2 % recovered, one lucky hit at 5.60) is the honest edge: at that depth the
search barely recovers anything, and the single survivor sits just above the cut. We do not
claim sensitivity there; 0.4 % is where recovery becomes real.

## Live E2E: five real stars, zero survivors

`run_monotransit` over every cached TESS star. This is the whole point of a monotransit
tool met by real data — and the honest result is **no candidate survives.**

```
 star            span    seg   events  surv | top event
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 TIC 102029734  2850d    12       1      0  | t0=4179.96 d=0.2 depth=0.24% sig=14.23  [edge:x coh:P fla:P sym:P]
 TIC 469906369  2879d    19       2      0  | t0=2085.10 d=0.8 depth=0.10% sig=11.68  [edge:x coh:P fla:P sym:x]
 TIC 253825414  2548d     7       1      0  | depth≈0  sig≈0                          [all P, not significant]
 TIC 7318157      25d     1       1      0  | depth≈0  sig=-0.07                       [all P, not significant]
 TIC 7187543      25d     1       0      0  | no event clears the structural floors
```

Two lessons, both load-bearing for sub-11-3:

1. **The significance cut alone is not enough — the discriminators are the second line.**
   `TIC 102029734` scores **sig = 14.23**, nearly 3× the threshold. If significance were
   the only gate it would be a candidate. It is not: its `t0` sits **0.066 d after a 2.5-day
   gap**, with ingress inside the gap, so the **edge** discriminator rejects it — segment
   boundaries (momentum dumps, downlink resets, sector joins) ring exactly there. Depth of
   defense caught what depth of significance let through. A monotransit hunt built on the
   significance cut alone would have cried wolf on the loudest thing in the sector.

2. **The two genuine single-sector curves produce nothing.** `TIC 7187543` and
   `TIC 7318157` (span 24.6 d each — one real sector) yield no candidate: 7187543 has no
   event clearing the structural floors, 7318157's best event is depth ≈ 0 noise
   (sig -0.07). The tool is quiet where there is nothing, which is the behavior a
   discovery claim has to rest on.

### The `TIC 102029734` deep dip, resolved

sub-11-1 carried it as a "single-transit anchor" (k = 1, held). Run through the monotransit
tool, its loud dip (sig 14.23, SES 93) is **not a real transit**: it is a segment-edge
artifact, rejected by the `edge` discriminator for the physical reason edges exist. The
"anchor" framing is corrected — it was a boundary ringing, not a planet crossing. The other
loud event, `TIC 469906369` sig 11.68, fails **both** edge and symmetry (a one-sided ramp no
central transit leaves). Note also these three "TESS" caches span thousands of days: they are
multi-sector concatenations, so their "segment edges" are sector joins — and that is precisely
where both false alarms landed.

## Honest boundary (carried forward)

The tool is a **single-transit search**, and its output is a "light-curve single-transit
candidate" — a lower bar than a TOI, never "planet" or "detection". The measured
sensitivity floor is ~0.4 % depth on a 24.6-day sector; shallower transits are not reliably
recovered and no claim is made there. The live sweep found zero survivors, which is the
correct and expected result on five stars that were never selected for having a lone
transit — the point of sub-11-2 was to build the brick and prove it stays quiet, not to
find a planet. sub-11-3 points it at stars chosen because a folded search came up empty,
and carries forward the load-bearing lesson: the significance cut is necessary but not
sufficient — the discriminators, edge first, are what stop the loudest false alarm.

---

## Result

The pipeline can now ask a question BLS cannot: *did something cross once?* It scores a lone
dip against correlated noise, holds judgment when the baseline is too thin to answer, and
guards the survivor with rules that each say why. Pointed at real data it stayed quiet —
and the one dip loud enough to clear the significance cut was caught, correctly, as a
segment-edge artifact. The brick is laid, and it does not lie.
