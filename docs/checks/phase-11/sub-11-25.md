## The lever, and why it might have worked

Levers 1–3 are settled (sub-11-23/24, vision §9-1): PDCSAP wins, the detrend window is flat against
the floor, and the targets are already multi-sector so stitching is on. The one knob left is the
**per-point scatter itself**. The naive premise is strong: bin N adjacent cadences and white noise
averages down by √N, so a 30-minute bin (~15 of the 2-min cadences) should cut the scatter to ~¼ and
let a shallower transit clear the significance cut. If any lever were going to lower these floors,
this is the one that touches the noise directly.

## The catch, measured not argued

The floor is not the raw per-point scatter. `sensitivity_depth_floor` reads the scatter of
**matched-filter depths** over 0.4-day control windows — `depth_hat = Σ(d·s) / Σ(s·s)`, a quantity
already integrated over ~288 cadences of the transit template. White noise is *already* beaten down by
that integration; what remains in the control-depth scatter is largely red (correlated) noise, which
binning cannot touch. So the prediction is that binning is redundant. We measured it rather than
resting on the argument.

## Method

`scripts/measure_binning_floor.py` downloads each target's PDCSAP curve once, then for each bin width
bins the raw curve (`bls._bin_curve`, 0 = unbinned control) and runs the *identical* rest of the
pipeline (detrend 30 d → baseline-1 → `sensitivity_depth_floor`), converting each floor to the
smallest planet that curve could reveal (√floor·R★, R⊕). Bins swept: 0 / 5 / 10 / 20 / 30 min.
Targets: path A's 11 Earth-size stars. A second, independent guard measures the *cost* — how much of a
synthetic transit's depth survives each bin.

sample, not a sensitivity gain. The prediction held: **binning is redundant because the floor already
integrates over the transit duration.**

## The cost guard — where binning would hurt if used blindly

| synthetic dip | 5m | 10m | 20m | 30m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 h | 100% | 100% | 100% | 92% |
| 6 h | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| 12 h | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% |

Long transits (the monotransit regime — a long-period planet's central crossing is many hours)
survive binning up to 20 min at full depth; a 30-min bin starts to smear a 2-hour dip (−8%). So
binning is not free: it would trade transit depth on the shortest dips for no floor gain. Another
reason to leave it off.

## Honest boundary

- Scoped to these 11 quiet dwarfs. On a much noisier star with large *white* systematics on the
  cadence timescale, binning could in principle help — but those are not path A's targets, whose whole
  point is that they are already quiet.
- As in sub-11-23/24 this measures the **noise floor** (the completeness yardstick), not whether a
  given bin erases a genuine long transit — the cost guard addresses that separately on synthetic dips.
