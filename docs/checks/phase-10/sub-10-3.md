## L4: what the calibration was for, and what it found

The two numbers governing short-baseline TESS shipped as admitted guesses:
`_N_MIN_TRANSITS` and the Savitzky-Golay window in `bls._detrend`. The bright
frontier pool could not exercise them, because the brightest unrecorded stars sit
near the ecliptic poles where TESS stares for many sectors — sub-10-4's E2E target
had 9 sectors and 2850 days of baseline. So L4a first had to *find* the regime.

Every defect below is the same shape: **a quantity measured against the wrong
denominator, which Kepler's continuous, coarsely-sampled staring hid and TESS
exposed.** None was visible in unit tests on synthetic flat curves.

### L4a — a genuinely single-sector target (`92babea`)

`scripts/find_single_sector_targets.py` keeps only TICs observed in exactly one SPOC
sector mission-wide, batching the MAST lookup as the Kepler seed builder does. Live:
sector 104, **18 of 100** unrecorded TICs are single-sector; brightest is
**TIC 7318157** (Tmag 8.57, Teff 4827 K, observed 2026-05-18).

### L4b/L4c — the detrend window was 2 days, not 30 (`00e943c`, `40e08f4`)

`_detrend` computed `int(30.0 / 0.0204)` = 1470 **points** — 30 days only at Kepler
long cadence. Injection-recovery on the real light curve (0.5% box transit, depth
recovered after detrending):

```
    window     points   segs    depth    kept
    (none)          -      -   0.480%   100.0%
    CURRENT      1470      ?   0.395%    82.2%   <- shipped code = 2.04 d here
       2.0d      1439    2/2   0.392%    81.8%
       5.0d      3599    2/2   0.460%    95.9%
      10.0d      7199    2/2   0.483%   100.7%
      15.0d     10799    0/2   0.480%   100.0%   <- SKIPPED, segment too short
```

The filter was removing ~18% of transit depth. And the obvious fix is a trap: 30 days
of 2-minute data is 21,599 points, longer than either half of a sector (sectors split
at the mid-sector downlink), so `if n < win: continue` would have left single-sector
curves **entirely undetrended**. The fix derives the window from measured cadence,
clamps it to the segment instead of skipping, and refuses to go below
`_MIN_WINDOW_DAYS = 5.0` — under the floor the filter costs more transit depth than
the trend it removes. Depth kept: **82.2% → 100.3%**.

`_detrend` also returned flux in sorted order while both callers pair it with their
own unsorted `time`; now it restores the caller's ordering. It had no tests at all
before this.

### L4d/L4e — the odd/even gate was rejecting real planets (`0fb967d`, `1919b66`)

Calibrating `_N_MIN_TRANSITS` by injecting planets and binaries into the real curve
showed clean planets being rejected 25–83% of the time — depth-independent, not
de-aliasing, and **worse with more transits**. Cause: `_group_depth` computed the
depth error as `_mad_std(flux_in) / sqrt(n)` with `n` counting in-transit *samples*,
treating ~108 correlated samples inside one TESS transit as independent draws (Kepler
long cadence gives ~22, so the same fallacy cost far less there). Sampling more
finely manufactured confidence; the stellar variability actually driving the odd/even
difference does not average down at all.

`_transit_depths` now collapses each transit to one depth and compares the two sides
with a pooled two-sample t statistic, so scatter *between* transits sets the error.
Below two transits per side the gate holds judgment rather than ruling.

```
                    planet kept          binary caught
                    before    after      before   after
    TIC 7318157     17-67%    92-100%    100%*    90%+ from N=8
    TIC 7187543       --      75-100%      --     90%+ from N=7

    * not discrimination — the old gate rejected almost everything, planets
      included. Removing the false alarms is what makes the real curve visible.
```

`_N_MIN_TRANSITS` therefore moves **5 → 8**, measured rather than assumed: 5 predated
a working gate. On a 24.6 d sector that makes everything past a ~2.9 d period
provisional, an honest description of what one sector supports.

### L4f — transit_count counted transits that never happened (`afb7be8`)

Found while re-running anchors: sub-10-4's E2E reported **93 transits,
provisional=False** while the odd/even gate on the same report found 0 odd and 1 even
transit *with data in them*. `transit_count` was `floor(baseline / period)`, counting
every epoch the star would have transited including all those falling in gaps. Its 9
sectors span 2850 days but the star is only pointed at for a few weeks of that, so 92
of the 93 happened where the light curve does not exist. `observed_transits()` now
counts epochs actually carrying data.

### L4g — sector_count counted products (`aded1bf`)

`len(search)` counted lightkurve rows. SPOC serves the same pointing at 20 s and
120 s, so TIC 7318157's single sector reported as two — on the very badge that tells
a hunter how thin the look was. The same unfiltered search also stitched mixed
cadences while the payload declared `exptime "short"`. Both fixed.

## Honest caveat — this `strong` is very probably noise (nail #3)

The E2E verdict above should not be read as a candidate, and the reason matters more
than the run:

- the BLS peak is 0.038% deep with power 0.0004 — marginal;
- `depth_odd` is essentially **zero** while `depth_even` carries the whole signal.
  The dip shows up in even epochs and not odd ones, a classic signature of noise
  rather than a body. The gate passes it only because the scatter is wide enough that
  sigma stays at 1.83.

The four gates ask *does this shape look planetary*, never *is this a real
detection*. Nothing in the pipeline gates on detection significance, so a marginal
BLS peak can walk through all four. This is a pre-existing limit (Phase 9, LC-only
vetting) that single-sector data makes far easier to hit, and it is the honest reason
a single sector cannot produce a candidate on its own. **Filed as a follow-up rather
than fixed here** — it needs a designed significance gate, not a patch.

Phase 10's front is complete: a discoverable hub, a mission-aware hunt, nail-4
